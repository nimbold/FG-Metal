# Step 10B.4 — DXMT cadence and presentation feedback

**Result: PARTIAL PASS.** The single-owner and SourceEscrow guarantees survived both scheduler changes, callback accounting is complete, and the worker submits close to every 60 Hz opportunity. The final advance-B candidate (I) reduced no-output ticks from 26 to 19 and raised positive `presentedTime` from 56.391/s in baseline F to 57.079/s. Its p95 positive interval remains 45.183 ms, so cadence is still below the requested behavior. The comparison is descriptive: F and I are single runs with different source rates, and I also had three owner epochs and naturally rejected submissions. In I, 1,582 positive G results have positive timestamps for both exact endpoints and those timestamps satisfy A < G < B; 269 positive G timestamps follow B's display target. The early-B policy also produces positive B timestamps before B's scheduled source time in many cases. These API timestamps do not prove panel scanout order.

**Decision:** Keep the local downstream DXMT route for further timing and feedback work. Do not begin RIFE. The 60-second synthetic gate did not pass the strict cadence and A/G/B evidence criteria, so the conditional 15-minute soak was not run. Highball and upstream DXMT were not changed.

## Scope and candidate identity

This step continued from the Step 10B.3 single-owner architecture. It kept one CAMetalLayer owner, same-command-buffer SourceEscrow, post-commit transfer, worker-only presents while active, and quiescent handoff. Candidate F made G eligible on the first display opportunity at or after the A/B midpoint minus 8.333 ms, through B’s scheduled source target. It did not enforce a symmetric upper half-period cutoff: one of 1,771 selected G opportunities was 19.704 ms after midpoint, though still 1.268 ms before B’s scheduled target. Candidate I adds a Phase 9 fallback: after G is selected, it selects the next committed source B on the next display opportunity, even when B's scheduled source target is still ahead. This avoids repeating A or leaving that opportunity empty while preserving selected source-ID order A, G, B. It does not wait for positive G feedback before advancing B. The policy changes presentation timing, not ownership, source acceptance, or the committed source frontier.

The downstream build is based on DXMT `fb4515681daefb789a4d0f403c4bdbca88f3b3de`, in disposable checkout `/private/tmp/fg-metal-step10b3-downstream`. Baseline F used `/private/tmp/dxmt-step10b4-run-v9/engine`; final candidate I used `/private/tmp/dxmt-step10b4-run-v10/engine`. Binary hashes, the controlled-app identity, and the source-tree hash are in [runtime-identity.txt](../../experiments/dxmt_framegen/evidence/step10b4/runtime-identity.txt). No upstream contribution or patch was prepared.

The controlled app made one ordinary DXGI Present per source frame at about 30 Hz. Tests ran on the visible, foreground window and a 60 Hz display. No screenshot, CPU pixel read, private API, or extra DXGI Present was used.

## Callback accounting and the cadence gap

For final I, the native ledger records 3,583 callbacks and 3,583 terminal classifications: 1,801 source submissions, 1,763 generated submissions, and 19 `no_output_available` ticks. The callback-minus-terminal delta is zero, there are no duplicate tick terminals, no queue overflows, and each tick has a unique ID. F recorded 3,601/3,601 terminal classifications with 26 no-output ticks. B and C also have zero ledger delta. Every callback gets a terminal classification in `presentation_stages`, including ticks that select no output.

In F, 21 of the 26 no-output ticks occurred after G was submitted while B was not yet due; repeating A would violate the committed A/G/B source order. The advance-B branch addresses that state by selecting B on the next callback after G selection. I recorded 19 no-output ticks total, down by seven from F despite four pre-transfer escrow setup failures and a higher app source rate. Two ticks were explicitly expired at the render deadline; the other no-output records remain classified by the ledger without a proven platform cause. These are distinct from I's 158 submitted drawable results with zero `presentedTime`.

The observed cadence loss is after callback delivery and submission. B, C, F, and I all delivered approximately 60 callbacks/s and submitted approximately 60 drawables/s. Source-only C still averaged 56.383 positive results/s, so generation is not required to produce the feedback gap. In I, all 3,564 submissions completed on the GPU; 158 results had zero `presentedTime` and 3,406 had positive `presentedTime`. The positive rate is 57.079/s and the p95 interval is 45.183 ms. GPU completion does not distinguish whether a zero-timestamp drawable was displayed or dropped.

There were 490 positive-result intervals longer than 25 ms in I. Of those, 118 had a zero-feedback result whose target fell within the gap. The other 372 contained no zero-feedback result in that target window and remain classified as positive-feedback spacing or target phase. The logs therefore place the recurring loss after submission and identify zero feedback as one contributor, but they do not identify the platform reason for every long interval.

The bridge receives asynchronous drawable feedback about 44.9 ms after callback at the median and about 73.4–76.1 ms at the p95–p99. The active generated-work slot is released at GPU completion, which can occur before the drawable feedback callback; generated presentation attempts may therefore overlap in feedback lifetime. Zero values are not sampled immediately after `present()`. Apple documents zero as indicating a drawable that has not been presented or whose frame was dropped; the observed API result alone cannot distinguish those outcomes or verify panel scanout. The implementation does not re-read zero-valued drawables later, so a later property transition was not measured.

## Display-link timing and generated readiness

The display link is created from the swapchain's CAMetalLayer, uses the same `MTLDevice`, requests a fixed 60/60/60 range and latency 1, runs on the main run loop's common modes, and is explicitly paused/enabled during lifecycle transitions. The standalone probe and foreground DXMT controls received about 60 callbacks/s. Final I received 60.045 callbacks/s and submitted 59.726 drawables/s. Configuration or callback delivery does not explain the roughly 2.97 positive results/s missing after submission.

Apple defines `targetTimestamp` as the render deadline and `targetPresentationTimestamp` as the system's estimated display time for the next frame. In the standalone probe, the median leads from callback time were 16.534 ms and 49.866 ms respectively, a 33.332 ms difference. The initial synthetic run selected 1,443 of 1,800 ready G pairs; its selected target was a median 15.67 ms after the requested midpoint. The midpoint-threshold rule used by F and I moved the median to 3.88 ms after midpoint. The selection window has no symmetric upper cutoff; in F, one selected G opportunity was 19.704 ms after midpoint and 1.268 ms before B’s scheduled target.

In final I, 1,788 G pairs were requested, 1,785 became ready, and 1,763 were selected and submitted. Of those submissions, 1,638 had positive feedback and 125 had zero feedback. The analyzer reports 22 unique requested pairs dropped before submission. Its 151 `generated_dropped` event records comprise 125 zero-`presentedTime` outcomes, 22 missed slots while B progressed, two generator-submit rejections, and two missed slots where A was repeated. A zero timestamp remains unclassified as displayed versus dropped. G submit-to-ready time was 14.305 ms p50 and 15.924 ms p95. The trace did not provide `history_copy_completion_ms`, so B escrow completion → G submit and completion → selected-tick lead cannot be calculated from this run. The two naturally rejected generator submissions occurred on source pairs (2,3) and (7,8) before the advance-B branch was first selected; both reported `interpolation fraction must match the supplied interpolation timestamp`. This rejection also appears in the earlier Step 10B.3 soak, so this run does not establish it as a regression from the policy change. All accepted sources remained safe.

The advance-B policy fills some ticks that F left empty, but its early timing is a trade-off. There were 152 post-G decisions to advance to B before B's scheduled source time. All 152 B submissions had positive feedback: 125 returned B timestamps before the scheduled source time (median 12.815 ms early, maximum 26.546 ms early), while 27 returned timestamps after it (median 15.136 ms late, maximum 16.789 ms late). In I, 269 of 1,638 positive G timestamps were after B's display target and 1,369 were before it; none were later than B's positive timestamp where that endpoint feedback was available. B positive feedback was unavailable for 28 G pairs. These are separate comparisons: B's scheduled source time, B's first attempt target, and B's positive `presentedTime` are not interchangeable.

Among I's 1,638 positive G results, 1,582 had positive feedback for both exact endpoints (96.6%). All 1,582 renderer-reported `presentedTime` values were ordered A < G < B: there were zero G-before-A and zero G-after-B observations. The remaining 56 lacked a complete positive endpoint bracket: 28 lacked A feedback and 28 lacked B feedback; none lacked both. These records do not establish physical panel order.

This separates target lateness from positive timestamp order: the observed endpoint triples are ordered by renderer-reported timestamps, but G timestamps after B's target, missing endpoint feedback, and B timestamps that precede B's scheduled source time prevent a strict A/G/B scheduling PASS. These API timestamps do not establish physical panel scanout order.

## Worker timing, Presenter cost, and drawable lifetime

Final I timing was low through the normal callback path: callback-to-worker wake was 0.050 / 0.207 / 0.731 ms p50/p95/p99; callback-to-selection was 0.128 / 0.714 / 1.308 ms; device-mutex wait was 0.002 ms p99; mutex hold was 0.956 / 1.989 / 3.774 ms; Presenter encode was 0.227 / 0.340 / 0.604 ms; GPU completion was 1.135 / 1.393 / 1.781 ms. Callback-to-present was 0.846 / 1.673 / 3.754 ms. Rare maxima included a 38 ms mutex hold and a 38 ms callback-to-selection interval; those can explain isolated misses, but not the persistent p95 interval tail.

The callback only timestamps, retains the supplied drawable, and enqueues a bounded record. The worker still scans bounded history/generated collections during selection, builds a `PresentationAttempt` per submitted drawable, and logs stage records on the critical path. This step did not introduce the proposed explicit `PreparedRealSource` / `PreparedGenerated` / `FallbackRealSource` slot or make selection allocation-free. Typical worker and Presenter timings do not explain the repeated feedback tail, so I did not expand the state machine around a prepared-slot cache; this remains a requested optimization that is **not implemented**. The rare callback-to-selection maxima leave an isolated worker-late contribution possible.

DXMT creates and stores its blit/scaling render pipeline states on Presenter setup and reuses them in the per-frame path. The controlled SDR run does not rebuild those pipelines on each tick. The display-link path uses the supplied drawable; the native owner guard prevents ordinary `nextDrawable()` use while Framegen owns the layer. Final I's callback queue-depth p99 was 1 and maximum 2, with no queue overflow. The recorded drawable high-water of 3 counts queued and worker-owned callback drawable leases; it excludes submissions awaiting asynchronous feedback. Reconstructing in-flight presentation-feedback attempts from `present_submit_ns` to `drawable_feedback_ns` gives a peak of 5 and zero remaining after drain. The attempt is captured by the registered handlers until the callbacks arrive, but it does not hold a drawable reference; the peak of 5 is not an exact count of framework-retained drawable objects. Feedback arrived at a median of about 44 ms, with no growing feedback-attempt backlog during the run.

No QoS or realtime scheduling change was made because callback wake, lock acquisition, encoding, and GPU completion percentiles do not explain the repeated 45 ms spacing.

## Lifecycle and failure regressions

The post-change 65-second lifecycle run made 1,951 app Presents at 30.023/s. Thirty non-failing occlusion statuses occurred during the minimized interval. Resize, disable/re-enable, focus loss/recovery, fullscreen enter/exit, swapchain recreation, and shutdown completed. Eight display-link owner cycles were attached and drained. All 1,722 accepted sources had safe terminal outcomes; there were zero lost IDs, unsafe outcomes, source regressions, or ownership assertions. Two history-frontier capacity failures cancelled before transfer. The 52.936 callback/s and 49.751 positive/s lifecycle rates include deliberate pauses and window changes and are not cadence acceptance measures.

| Injected failure | Result |
|---|---|
| Escrow preflight | All 301 reservations were cancelled before ownership transfer; no source was accepted by Framegen, and all app Presents returned success. The injection caused 300 attach/release cycles and zero display callbacks. This demonstrates pre-transfer cancellation and no competing-owner assertion, but does not prove sustained visible fallback output. |
| Generator submission | All 300 accepted sources had safe terminal outcomes. The optional generator circuit opened; source submissions continued at 59.544/s and positive feedback at 57.539/s. |
| Generated completion | All 300 accepted sources had safe terminal outcomes; six were submitted but unconfirmed. No G became ready. The callback ledger balanced at 602/602; this run checks failure handling, not cadence acceptance. |
| Generation deadline | All 300 accepted sources were safe. All 300 requested G pairs became ready but were not selected or submitted; 300 were dropped before submit. The source path continued with 595 submissions and 571 positive feedback results; the callback ledger balanced at 600/600. |

Each failure run's callback ledger balanced where callbacks were delivered. None reported a source-safety violation, source regression, or ordinary-owner assertion. A true Metal device-wide failure, failure of the shared app-render/SourceEscrow command buffer, and a forced quiesce timeout were not injected. Source inspection also identifies an untested worker-exception window after `reportPresentation` registers callbacks and before `reportPresentationSubmitted` records the committed/presented drawable; a forced exception in that window was not run, so cleanup and owner handoff for it remain unverified. There is no claim of recovery from a device-wide failure. When preflight failure could not immediately quiesce around pending feedback, the code kept the layer claim rather than authorizing a competing drawable request.

The direct `CAMetalDrawable.presentAtTime` experiment terminated with `CAMetalDrawableInvalidOperation` stating that it must not be called with CAMetalDisplayLink. The public command-buffer `presentDrawable:atTime:` experiment accepted calls but stalled after six app Presents; it was terminated and retained as a failed method experiment. Final runs use the known-good `commit()` followed by `drawable.present()` path.

- The 15-minute soak was skipped because final I still has materially low positive feedback and unresolved endpoint/target feedback. The request conditions that gate the soak were not met.
- Six independent GPT-6 Luna reviews covered timing, display-link semantics, Presenter/drawable lifetime, scheduler chronology, feedback interpretation, and source-safety regression. They found no evidence that changes the PARTIAL PASS decision. They agree that positive `presentedTime` establishes renderer-reported timing only, not physical panel scanout; review findings were incorporated above.

Apple API references: [CAMetalDisplayLink](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametaldisplaylink), [Update.targetTimestamp](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametaldisplaylink/update/targettimestamp), [Update.targetPresentationTimestamp](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametaldisplaylink/update/targetpresentationtimestamp), and [MTLDrawable.presentedTime](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtldrawable/presentedtime).

**Final status: PARTIAL PASS.** The architecture remained source-safe, callback accounting is complete, and presentation submission remains near 60/s. The measured source of the lost positive feedback is downstream of submission and GPU completion; public `presentedTime` semantics do not tell which remaining result was displayed or skipped, and over three quarters of the long positive-feedback gaps lack an explicit zero result in their target window (372 of 490). The advance-B fallback reduced empty ticks but did not resolve the 45 ms p95 interval, and it scheduled B early relative to B's source target in 125 of 152 positive outcomes. I had 269 G timestamps after B's display target and 56 positive G results without a complete positive endpoint bracket. RIFE is not justified by this evidence.
